SecondBreath

Every breath out carries about 38,000 ppm of CO2, so the CO2 in a room above the outdoor level tells you how much of the air has already been through someone else's lungs.

Rebreathed fraction = (indoor − 420 ppm outdoor) ÷ 38,000 ppm (Rudnick & Milton, 2003). The highest reading in the lecture-hall dataset is 4,957 ppm, about 1 breath in 8.

The finding

Three university lecture halls in Limassol, Cyprus, each designed for 100% fresh air at about six air changes per hour, measured for a full academic year (2023–24).

Teaching hours (Mon–Fri 08:00–17:59) only. "Fits" are separate stretches of the CO2 record the solver fitted; the band is the middle half of those fits. "Uncertain" means the fits scatter too widely to quote as a headline. "Thin" means fewer than ten fits. Discarded buildup fits could not pin the rate down and are never quoted. Figures are exactly as in analysis/audit.json.

Why two methods, and why they matter

The decay fit reads the room as it empties. That invites an objection: if the air handler switches off when the lecture ends, decay measures the building's leakage, not the ventilation people get. The buildup fit answers it by reading the same room while it is full. If the system were delivering its design airflow during lectures, buildup would come out near six. It does not.

Fingerprint your own room

Upload a CO2 log (CSV with a timestamp column and a CO2 column). We measure the air-change rate two ways: from how fast CO2 falls once people leave (decay), and from how it rises while they are in the room (buildup).

Fits only weekday 08:00–17:59 data, in the file's own clock time, using the same rule as the audit above.

What this project is not

  • Not medical advice, and not an absolute infection probability. Rebreathed fraction is an exposure metric.
  • The decay method measures the room while it is emptying, so on its own it could be reporting infiltration rather than delivered ventilation. The buildup fit is the test of that, and both results are reported either way.
  • Single-point sensors assume a well-mixed room. Local exposure near a source can be higher than the room average.
  • Occupancy is not recorded in these datasets. The buildup fit infers an implied occupancy from the fitted source strength; that is an output of the model, not a measurement. This page does not show it.
  • Low-cost sensors drift. Readings below outdoor level are dropped, not clamped, and the drop count is reported.
  • Evidence that elevated CO2 directly impairs cognition is contested: Harvard's COGfx study found large effects at 1,400 ppm, a Danish study found none at 5,000 ppm. This page leads with rebreathed air for that reason.
  • No figure derived from an assumption that is not in the data is shown here. We do not estimate seat counts by dividing room volume by a made-up number.
  • A fit marked "uncertain" is shown struck through and must not be read as a headline. A buildup fit that cannot pin the rate down is discarded and never quoted.
