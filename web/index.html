SecondBreath

Every breath out carries about 38,000 ppm of CO2, so the CO2 in a room above the outdoor level tells you how much of the air has already been through someone else's lungs.

Rebreathed fraction = (indoor − 420 ppm outdoor) ÷ 38,000 ppm (Rudnick & Milton, 2003). The highest reading in the lecture-hall dataset is 4,957 ppm, about 1 breath in 8.

What this is not

  • Not medical advice, and not an infection probability. Rebreathed fraction measures exposure.
  • The decay method measures the room as it empties. If the air handler switches off when people leave, decay shows the building's leakage, not the ventilation delivered. The buildup fit, taken during occupancy, tests this, and both results are shown either way.
  • A single sensor assumes the air is well mixed. Air close to a person or a group can be worse than the room average.
  • These datasets do not record occupancy. Any occupancy the buildup fit implies is a model output, not a measurement.
  • Low-cost sensors drift. Readings below the outdoor level are dropped, not clamped, and the drop count is shown.
  • Whether high CO2 itself impairs thinking is contested. Harvard's COGfx study found large effects at 1,400 ppm, while a Danish study found none at 5,000 ppm. This tool reports rebreathed air, not cognition.
  • A buildup fit whose rate cannot be pinned down is discarded and never quoted. A result marked "uncertain" has widely scattered fits and should not be read as a headline.
